The warm-start plus Double DQN reached 91.45% optimal moves and won 56.0% against the optimal defender. It began from the supervised checkpoint; these results do not measure RL trained from scratch.

Held-out solver move quality and game W/D/L answer different questions. The 10,000-position move sample is distinct from the game results; read both metrics together rather than treating move accuracy as win rate.

Learning to convert KRvK

A small, reproducible study of reinforcement learning for King + Rook versus King. The attacker seeks checkmate; the lone-king defender resists.

5 MARKS

Problem definition & RL formulation

KRvK is a fully observable, sequential decision problem with many illegal nominal moves and sparse terminal outcomes. It is small enough for an exact tablebase to provide labels and an objective evaluation reference.

EnvironmentWhite K+R attacks; Black K defends. A training turn is an attacker move followed by a defender reply. Games end on checkmate, draw, or the 50-move limit.
StateDQN: 193 values for three piece planes plus move count. Dueling agents: 18 × 8 × 8 side-to-move planes.
ActionDQN: 36 compact moves. Dueling agents: from-square × 64 + to-square, with a legal-move mask.
RewardDQN: +10 checkmate (+speed bonus), −5 stalemate, −8 rook capture, −0.02 per step, +0.3 for check, king-distance shaping, and −3 at the move limit. Double-DQN fine-tuning uses +1 checkmate, −1 loss, 0 draw, −0.001 per step, and optional potential shaping.
Why RL?To study sequential decisions, delayed outcomes, and constrained actions in a controlled task with known ground truth. The solved endgame is a research testbed, not evidence RL beats the solver.

Implementation & working RL agent

Three selectable agents show different learning stages; only the DQN baseline learns from reward from scratch. The warm-start is supervised learning from tablebase labels, and Double DQN fine-tunes that checkpoint.

AgentMethodRole in the project
DQNReward-trained from scratchRL baseline
Dueling warm-startSupervised tablebase imitationReference policy; not an RL result
Warm-start + Double DQNReward-driven fine-tuningTests whether RL improves the reference policy

DQN learns with reward feedback and replay; the warm-start imitates tablebase labels; Double DQN fine-tunes it with reward feedback. Hyperparameters are documented defaults; no systematic tuning sweep was run. The checkpoints are included, so training is not required to run the demo.
